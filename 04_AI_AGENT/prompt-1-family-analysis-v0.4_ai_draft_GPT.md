Important differences between these senses
1. Affective computing versus emotional branding

Affective computing concerns the technological capacity to recognise, interpret or express emotion. Emotional branding concerns the strategic creation of emotional meaning and relationships between brands and consumers.

They are connected but not interchangeable.

2. Emotion recognition versus artificial empathy

Emotion recognition attempts to infer an individual's emotional state from available signals. Artificial empathy concerns the system's ability to produce a response perceived as understanding or acknowledging that emotional state.

A system can generate an empathetic-sounding message without accurately recognising anybody's emotions.

[checked] Huang and Rust's 2023 The Caring Machine: Feeling AI for Customer Care explicitly separates emotion recognition, emotional understanding, emotion management and emotional connection. 
Ming-Hui Huang, Roland T. Rust, 2024


3. Emotional AI versus generative AI

Generative AI can produce emotional language, images, stories and advertisements without possessing an emotion-recognition mechanism.

Consequently, a study of consumer reactions to AI-written emotional messages is relevant to the wider family but is not automatically a study of affective computing.

4. Emotional AI versus consumer-brand attachment

Consumer-brand attachment is an established marketing psychology construct. It describes consumers' emotional bonds with brands, not the technological capacity of AI.

It becomes relevant when examining whether AI-mediated communication influences emotional relationships with brands.

[checked] Thomson, MacInnis and Park's 2005 foundational work develops a measurement scale for consumer emotional attachment to brands. 
ScienceDirect

Are the senses related?
Overlapping
They are overlapping rather than unrelated.

They share technologies, theoretical concepts and research questions:
- Machine learning and natural language processing.
- Emotion theories and emotional expression.
- Human–computer interaction and social presence.
- Consumer behaviour and relationship marketing.
- Trust, authenticity, privacy and transparency.
However, their research objects differ. One may investigate the accuracy of facial emotion recognition, another the perceived empathy of a chatbot, and another consumers' trust in AI-written brand messages.

These must not be treated as identical research questions.

C. Legitimate borrowings
These terms should not be excluded blindly.

1. Psychology borrowed by affective computing

Affective computing uses psychological theories and emotional categories to develop computational approaches to emotion recognition.

Emotion labels such as happiness, sadness, anger and surprise can appear in both psychological and computational research.

2. Affective computing borrowed by advertising research

Advertising research increasingly uses computational emotion models to analyse emotional reactions to promotional content.

Wen et al. (2024), in Infusing Affective Computing Models into Advertising Research on Emotions, explicitly discuss incorporating affective computing into advertising research across text, visual and audio modalities. 
Taylor & Francis Online+1


3. Relationship marketing borrowed by artificial empathy research

Research on AI customer care borrows ideas from emotional connection, relationship building and emotional labour.

This helps explain why an AI interaction may matter beyond solving a customer's immediate problem.

4. Brand attachment borrowed by AI communication research

Consumer-brand attachment and brand love provide theoretical frameworks for investigating the longer-term consequences of AI-mediated communication.

These constructs are outcomes or explanatory frameworks, not necessarily technologies.

5. Social presence and anthropomorphism borrowed by brand communication

Research on AI brand personalities draws on human–computer interaction concepts to explain why conversational and socially expressive systems can influence consumer perceptions of brands.

9 RESEARCH AREAS
1. Affective Computing and Multimodal Emotion Recognition
Core discipline: Computer science, artificial intelligence, human–computer interaction and affective science.

This is the technological foundation of the family. It investigates how AI systems identify or infer human emotional states from different sources, including facial expressions, speech, language, physiological signals and combinations of these modalities. It also investigates how AI systems can model or express emotional behaviour.

Important figure and foundational work: Rosalind W. Picard, Affective Computing (1997) — background.

Important vocabulary: Emotion recognition, affect recognition, facial expression analysis, multimodal emotion detection, speech emotion recognition, physiological signals.

[checked] Picard's work provides a foundational point of reference for affective computing. The 2024 systematic review by García-Hernández et al. updates the technological landscape across multiple modalities. 
MIT Press+2


Relationship to other subtopics: It provides the technological foundation for emotion-aware advertising, affective personalization and AI customer care. It does not necessarily investigate brand communication itself.
2. Emotion-Aware Advertising and Computational Advertising
Core discipline: Advertising, computational marketing and consumer neuroscience.

This subtopic studies how AI can measure, analyse and predict audience emotional reactions to advertisements. The technologies may evaluate facial expressions, textual responses, audio content and physiological activity to understand how consumers react to advertising messages.

Research questions include whether positive emotion predicts advertisement liking, whether particular emotional sequences influence brand perception, and how emotional reactions relate to purchase intentions.

Important works and figures:
- McDuff et al. (2015), Predicting Ad Liking and Purchase Intent: Large-Scale Analysis of Facial Responses to Ads — background.
- Wen et al. (2024), Infusing Affective Computing Models into Advertising Research on Emotions.
[checked] The 2024 Wen et al. article directly connects affective computing models with advertising research and examines text, visual and audio analysis. Earlier work by McDuff and colleagues demonstrated the application of automatically coded facial responses to advertising effectiveness. 
Taylor & Francis Online+1


Relationship to other subtopics: It applies the technological methods of Subtopic 1 to advertising. It also connects with consumer neuroscience and research on consumer emotional responses.
3. AI-Based Sentiment Analysis and Brand Social Listening
Core discipline: Natural language processing, computational linguistics and digital marketing.

This research investigates how AI analyses consumer language across social networks, online reviews, comments and brand communities.

It distinguishes positive, negative and neutral sentiment and, in more advanced applications, identifies specific emotional categories. Companies can use the findings to monitor brand reputation, understand audience attitudes and identify communication problems.

Key theoretical and methodological areas:
- Natural language processing.
- Opinion mining.
- Sentiment classification.
- Emotion lexicons.
- Text-based emotion recognition.
- Automated brand reputation monitoring.
Important literature: García-Hernández et al. (2024), A Systematic Literature Review of Modalities, Trends, and Limitations in Emotion Recognition, Affective Computing, and Sentiment Analysis.

[checked] The 2024 review treats sentiment analysis and emotion recognition as closely related but distinguishable research areas.

4. Affective Personalization and Emotion-Based Marketing
Core discipline: Consumer analytics, computational marketing and personalised communication.

This subtopic examines how AI uses emotional information or inferred consumer states to adapt messages, recommendations and communication strategies.

Examples include:
- Adapting advertising creative to audience reactions.
- Adjusting communication tone.
- Identifying emotional responses to particular brand messages.
- Developing context-sensitive recommendations.
- Adjusting customer engagement strategies.
Important theoretical vocabulary: Affective personalization, emotional targeting, adaptive marketing, contextual advertising, dynamic creative optimization, predictive consumer analytics.

Important research foundations: Picard's affective computing framework and the advertising research reviewed by Wen et al. (2024).

[checked] Recent research on affective computational advertising identifies opportunities to apply multimodal emotion models to advertising analysis and message optimisation.

Relationship to other subtopics: Builds on emotion-recognition and consumer analytics methods. It also raises questions about privacy, manipulation, personalisation and consumer autonomy.
5. Artificial Empathy and Emotionally Intelligent Customer Care
Core discipline: Marketing, human–AI interaction and service research.

This subtopic studies whether AI systems can recognise emotional signals in customer interactions and respond in ways that consumers perceive as empathetic.

Examples include AI-powered customer-service assistants, conversational agents, service-recovery messages and emotionally sensitive customer support.

An important distinction is between actually understanding another person's emotional state and generating language that appears emotionally appropriate.

9. Critical Studies of Emotional AI: Bias, Validity, Privacy and Ethics
Core discipline: Critical AI studies, science and technology studies, affective science and AI governance.

This subtopic investigates whether AI can reliably identify emotions and whether using automated emotion inference creates problems involving discrimination, privacy, surveillance and consumer manipulation.

Important questions include:
- Can facial expressions reliably indicate internal emotional states?
- Do AI emotion-recognition systems reproduce social and cultural biases?
- Can consumer emotions be collected and interpreted without meaningful awareness?
- Is emotional persuasion by AI ethically acceptable?
- How should people be informed when brands use emotion-recognition technologies?
Important works:
- Cross, Acevedo and Hunter (2023), A Critique of Automated Approaches to Code Facial Expressions: What Do Researchers Need to Know?
- García-Hernández et al. (2024), systematic review of emotion recognition, affective computing and sentiment analysis.
[checked] Cross and colleagues identify significant concerns involving validity, reliability, gender and skin-colour biases, and difficulties interpreting facial expressions in non-idealised conditions. 
PubMed+1


Relationship to other subtopics: This is a cross-cutting critical perspective. It challenges the assumptions and practices used throughout emotion recognition, advertising measurement, customer care and affective personalization.

Important research debates
Debate 1: Can AI actually recognise emotion?

Some computational approaches assume that observable signals can provide meaningful information about emotional states. Critical affective science questions whether facial expression, voice or other signals can reliably establish what someone feels.

Debate 2: Is artificial empathy genuine empathy?

An AI system can generate an emotionally appropriate response without necessarily experiencing or understanding emotions in a human sense. Researchers distinguish perceived empathy from actual human emotional experience.

Debate 3: Does AI improve or damage brand relationships?

AI can make communication more personalised and efficient, but perceived inauthenticity or the loss of human involvement can weaken consumer responses.

Debate 4: Are emotional data suitable for commercial persuasion?

Emotion-informed communication might improve message relevance while also creating problems involving privacy, consumer autonomy and manipulation.
